# The Practical Guide to Audio Engineering

### From Vibrating Air to a Finished Mix — How Sound, Gear, and DAWs Actually Work

A hands-on book for musicians, home-recordists and the curious. It explains
*why* things sound the way they do — not just which knob to turn — covering
digital audio fundamentals, the guitar/amp signal chain (preamps, cabinets,
IRs and Neural Amp Modeler), mixing with EQ and effects for real instruments,
and a friendly introduction to the REAPER DAW.

---

## Who this book is for

You play an instrument, you have an audio interface (or want one), and you keep
running into terms like *44.1 kHz*, *impulse response*, *NAM*, *high-pass
filter* or *bus routing* without ever getting a straight explanation. This book
gives you the mental models first, then the practical settings.

No prior engineering knowledge is assumed. Where math helps intuition, it's
kept light and always tied back to something you can hear.

---

## How to read it

- **Part I** builds the foundation: what sound is and how computers store it.
  Read it first — everything else leans on it.
- **Part II** follows an electric-guitar signal from string to speaker, and
  explains the modern digital tone tools (IR and NAM). Bassists and keyboard
  players will still learn the core ideas here.
- **Part III** is the mixing toolkit: EQ and effects, with per-instrument
  recipes for guitar, bass, keyboard and vocals.
- **Part IV** gets you producing in REAPER.
- **Appendices** are quick-reference: a frequency chart, a glossary, and a
  recommended-reading list.

Each chapter ends with a **"Try this"** box — small experiments that turn
reading into ears-on understanding.
