# Chapter 11 — Effects Recipes by Instrument & Vocals

This is the "what do I actually put on it?" chapter. For each instrument it gives
a **signal chain** (in order) plus **starting settings**, pulling together the EQ
map (Chapter 9) and the effects toolkit (Chapter 10). 

> **Read this first.** These are *starting points* for a typical pop/rock
> context, not laws. Genre, arrangement and the specific recording change
> everything. Always: **subtract before you add, process in the full mix, and
> trust your ears over any number here.** Reverb/delay should live on **sends**
> (Chapter 10.5), not inserts.

---

## 11.1 Electric guitar

**Chain:** `Gate → EQ (cut) → Compression (light) → EQ (tone) → [Amp/NAM + IR if not already tracked] → sends: Delay, Reverb`

- **Gate** (esp. high-gain): threshold just above the noise floor to silence hiss
  between riffs (Chapter 5 gain = noise). Fast-ish release.
- **EQ:** HPF ~80–100 Hz; cut mud ~300–500 Hz if congested; preserve the
  200 Hz–1 kHz body; gentle presence boost ~3–5 kHz; tame fizz with a high-shelf
  cut above ~7 kHz if harsh (better: fix the IR/mic, Chapter 6).
- **Compression:** light — ratio ~2:1–4:1, a few dB reduction, medium attack to
  keep pick attack. Clean rhythm/funk benefits more; heavily distorted guitar is
  already compressed by the distortion, so go easy.
- **Effects:**
  - **Overdrive/distortion** = the amp/NAM itself (Chapters 5, 8).
  - **Delay:** tempo-synced 1/8 or dotted-1/8 for leads/ambience; low feedback
    (2–4 repeats), moderate level. Slapback for rockabilly.
  - **Reverb:** spring or room for vintage; plate/hall for leads. Keep rhythm
    parts fairly dry; feature leads more.
  - **Modulation:** chorus/phaser on clean parts for shimmer; flanger for
    special moments.
- **Doubling:** record two takes, pan hard L/R for a wide, powerful rhythm wall
  (more effective than chorus-faking width).

## 11.2 Acoustic guitar

**Chain:** `EQ (cut) → Compression (gentle) → EQ (sparkle) → sends: Reverb (+ light Delay)`

- **EQ:** HPF ~70–100 Hz; cut boom ~150–300 Hz; cut boxiness ~500 Hz–1 kHz if
  "honky"; gentle air shelf ~8–12 kHz for shimmer; presence ~2–5 kHz for pick
  detail if it needs to cut.
- **Compression:** gentle, ~2:1–3:1, slow-ish attack to keep the percussive pluck,
  to even out strumming dynamics.
- **Effects:** light plate or room **reverb** for air (high-pass the return!); a
  subtle 1/8 **delay** can add space to fingerpicked parts. A touch of **chorus**
  for a lush singer-songwriter feel. As a solo/lead instrument it can take more
  reverb; in a dense mix keep it drier and let it occupy ~2–5 kHz and the air
  band.

## 11.3 Bass

**Chain:** `EQ (clean up) → Compression (firm) → [Saturation] → [Parallel distortion] → EQ (definition)`

- **EQ:** HPF very low (~30–40 Hz) to remove sub-rumble without losing weight;
  the power is ~80–200 Hz; cut boom ~180–250 Hz if flubby; boost
  **definition/growl ~700 Hz–2.5 kHz** so it's audible on phones/laptops. Carve
  around the **kick** (decide who owns the deep lows).
- **Compression:** **firmer than most instruments** — ratio ~4:1, a few dB+
  reduction, medium attack/medium-fast release — to keep a steady, even
  foundation. Bass benefits from consistency more than dynamics.
- **Saturation:** gentle tube/tape saturation adds harmonics that make bass
  **translate on small speakers** (you "hear" the bass even where the lows can't
  reproduce — same principle as the definition boost). Very effective.
- **Parallel distortion** (a distorted send blended under the clean) is a classic
  rock/metal trick for grind and presence while keeping the clean low end.
- **Generally avoid** reverb/delay/heavy modulation on bass — they smear the low
  end and muddy the mix. Keep bass tight, focused, mono in the lows.

## 11.4 Keyboard / piano / synth

**Chain:** `EQ → Compression (to taste) → [Modulation] → sends: Reverb, Delay`

- **EQ:** focus on **50–250 Hz** for warmth vs. mud; HPF bright pads/organs/leads
  so they don't crowd bass; cut low-mid mud ~200–500 Hz; presence ~3–6 kHz for a
  piano/lead to come forward; air shelf ~10–14 kHz for polish. Wide instruments
  mask vocals — **carve a dip where the vocal sits.**
- **Compression:** acoustic/electric piano benefits from gentle leveling
  (~2:1–3:1); pads usually need little; plucky synths can take more for
  consistency.
- **Effects:**
  - **Reverb:** hall/plate suits piano and pads for lushness; keep leads tighter.
  - **Delay:** great on electric piano (Rhodes) and synth leads/arps; tempo-
    synced.
  - **Modulation:** **chorus** is iconic on Rhodes/electric piano and synth pads
    (width/shimmer); **phaser** on Rhodes/clavinet for funk; tremolo on electric
    piano/organ.
- **Stereo:** synths/pads can be spread wide; keep deep-bass synths mono-centered.

## 11.5 Vocals (lead)

**Chain:** `Gate/cleanup → EQ (subtractive) → Compression → De-esser → EQ (tone/air) → [Saturation] → sends: Delay, Reverb`

- **EQ (subtractive):** **HPF ~80–120 Hz** (higher for thin voices); cut
  chest/mud ~200–400 Hz; cut boxiness ~500 Hz–1 kHz if needed.
- **Compression:** vocals have huge dynamic range, so compress **firmly, often in
  two stages**: a first compressor ~3:1–4:1 catching peaks, optionally a second
  gentler one for consistency. Aim for an even, upfront vocal where every word is
  audible. Medium attack (keep consonant clarity), auto/medium release.
- **De-esser:** tame sibilance ~5–9 kHz (Chapter 10) — *after* compression
  (which can exaggerate "s"). Don't just EQ-cut it (dulls the voice).
- **EQ (additive):** presence/intelligibility boost ~3–6 kHz; **air shelf
  ~10–16 kHz** for the "expensive" sheen; small warmth bump ~100–200 Hz if thin
  (watch mud).
- **Saturation:** a little adds density and helps the vocal cut without raw
  volume.
- **Effects (on sends):**
  - **Delay:** the main width/depth tool for vocals — keeps clarity better than
    heavy reverb. Tempo-synced 1/4 or 1/8, modest feedback. A **slapback**
    (~80–120 ms) thickens. **Haas** (<40 ms L/R) widens. Pro trick: a delay that
    only blooms in the *gaps* between phrases.
  - **Reverb:** plate is the classic vocal reverb. Use **pre-delay (~20–60 ms)**
    so the dry vocal stays in front and intelligible; **high-pass and low-pass
    the reverb return** so it doesn't muddy; consider **ducking** the reverb under
    the vocal (compress the return, keyed by the vocal) so it swells in the
    gaps — keeps lyrics clear while sounding lush.
  - **Combine** a little reverb with delay rather than drowning in reverb alone.

## 11.6 Backing vocals

- EQ similar to lead but you can **carve more** (cut some presence ~3–5 kHz) so
  they sit *behind* the lead and don't compete.
- **More effects than the lead:** more reverb, more delay, heavier **chorus** for
  width, harder compression for a smooth blended bed.
- **Double/triple and pan wide** for a big, lush stack; the lead stays centered
  and drier so it reads as the focus.

## 11.7 A universal reminder

Effects are seasoning. The order of priority is always: **good source → good
arrangement → levels/balance → panning → EQ → compression → *then* time/space
effects.** If something sounds wrong, fix it earlier in that list before adding
another plugin. The best mixers use **fewer, more intentional** moves — not more
plugins.

## Summary

- **Electric guitar:** gate (if high-gain) → clean-up EQ → light comp → amp/NAM
  + IR → modest delay/reverb on leads; double-track for width.
- **Acoustic:** HPF + de-boom/de-box EQ → gentle comp → air → light reverb.
- **Bass:** HPF low → firm compression → saturation for translation → definition
  boost ~700 Hz–2.5 kHz; **avoid time effects**, keep lows mono.
- **Keys/synth:** manage 50–250 Hz warmth/mud, carve for the vocal; chorus on
  Rhodes/pads; reverb/delay to taste.
- **Vocals:** HPF → subtractive EQ → firm (often two-stage) compression →
  de-esser → presence/air EQ → delay (primary) + EQ'd, pre-delayed, ducked
  reverb on sends. Backing vocals: more effects, panned wide, sitting behind.
- Effects are **seasoning** — fix problems earlier in the chain first.

---

> **Try this.** Build a vocal chain in this exact order on one take and bypass
> each stage in turn: hear the HPF clear the rumble, the compression even the
> words, the de-esser tame the "s," the air shelf add sheen, the delay add space.
> Then disable them all and re-enable only the *three* that helped most. That
> "less is more" pass is how pro mixes stay clean.
